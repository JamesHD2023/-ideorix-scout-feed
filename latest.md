1. 🤖 A seven-figure debut deal just collapsed over suspected AI — with no proof either way

A crime debut that won a reported 14-way auction at Minotaur — six- and seven-figure deals across the US and UK — was withdrawn by the author's own agents late last week (reported 30–31 July) after early readers flagged what they called hallmarks of AI prose: a flat style, off-kilter metaphors. The agents had accepted the author's assurance that no AI was used, then pulled the book saying they could no longer substantiate it; the author denies using AI. No detector verdict, no admission — suspicion alone was enough to unwind the biggest debut deal of the year, and the trade press is openly asking what "AI guardrails" even look like now.

2. Think local first: the book publicity most authors skip

A guest piece on Jane Friedman's blog (30 July) makes the case that local publicity — bookshops, libraries, community press, regional radio — should come BEFORE the national push, because readers buy books from authors they feel connected to, and connection is easiest to build where you live. Practical, low-cost, and honest about why the big-outlet chase so often returns nothing for a debut.

🔗 https://janefriedman.com/why-you-should-focus-on-local-book-publicity-first/
