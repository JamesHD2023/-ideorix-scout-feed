1. UK publishers ask the regulator to rein in Amazon — the market where most indie sales live

The Publishers Association's chief executive Dan Conway has called on the UK's Competition and Markets Authority to give Amazon "Strategic Market Status" — the designation that lets the regulator impose conduct rules on companies with entrenched market power. His case, made in a speech covered by the trade press this week (The Bookseller and Publishing Perspectives, w/c 20 July): Amazon holds roughly 50 percent of the UK print book market and 90 percent of digital and audiobook sales, and a survey of 150 publishers alleged practices including removed buy buttons, false out-of-stock markings and de-listing threats during negotiations. Whatever comes of it, indie authors are the most Amazon-dependent publishers of all — any UK conduct rules on fair terms and self-preferencing would touch every KDP author selling into Britain.

2. ALLi on the "Know, Like and Trust" framework — marketing for authors who hate marketing

The Alliance of Independent Authors' latest advice video (23 July) walks through the Know, Like & Trust framework: readers buy from authors they have come to know, warmed to, and trust — so marketing is less about shouting into the feed and more about steadily becoming familiar and credible to a defined readership. 3. Sarra Cannon starts a public "100 days" push — accountability as craft fuel

Heart Breathings' Sarra Cannon launched PROJECT 180 on Friday (25 July): 100 days left in her personal challenge to change her writing life, documented in public on the channel. Whatever you think of public goal-setting, her virtual writing retreats and sprint videos have quietly become one of the most-used accountability tools in the indie community — and the second half of a year is exactly when writing goals go to die.
